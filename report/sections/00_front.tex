\textbf{Abstract.}
This memoir studies two reward-free approaches to imitation learning.
The first matches state-occupancy measures with $f$-divergences: $f$-IRL learns a reward through a maximum-entropy inner loop, and $f$-policy gradients ($f$-PG) differentiate the divergence directly with respect to the policy.
The second learns, without rewards, a forward--backward (FB) representation of successor measures once, and imitates by choosing a task vector: \ERFB{} reads it off the demonstrations and \DMFB{} fits it by matching successor measures.
We write both families in one notation, prove their central results under explicit assumptions, and separate the exact statements from the approximations that the algorithms add: plug-in density estimates, finite rank, sampling, truncation and smoothing.
We adapt $f$-PG to a fixed expert distribution and derive its gradient, estimator and baselines; we show that for softmax policies the FB optimality theorem is replaced by a temperature bound, and that the \DMFB{} loss expansion holds without greedy policies.
We then compare $f$-PG with FB imitation in two $7\times7$ finite mazes, where an exact oracle evaluates every policy, and in a continuous four-rooms maze, with three seeds and a protocol frozen before the final runs.
In the finite mazes, all \vn{abs.fpg.n}{18} $f$-PG runs reach the predeclared thresholds (held-out occupancy Jensen--Shannon divergence at most $0.10$ nats and success at least $0.80$), first at the $\vn{abs.fpg.first.lo}{20{,}000}$- or $\vn{abs.fpg.first.hi}{30{,}000}$-transition evaluation checkpoint (checkpoints every $\vn{fpg.interval.f}{10{,}000}$ transitions), whereas at the end of the budget \ERFB{} meets them in \vn{abs.er.last}{2} of \vn{n.runs.finite}{18} runs and \DMFB{} in \vn{abs.dm.last}{none}.
\DMFB{}'s model objective decreased in all \vn{abs.dm.loss_down}{27} adaptations, while the held-out occupancy divergence increased in \vn{abs.dm.js_up}{20} of them (in \vn{abs.dm.js_up_finite}{16} of the \vn{abs.dm.n_finite}{18} finite ones, where it is computed exactly).
In the continuous maze no method reaches the thresholds within its budget; $f$-PG ends closest (divergence $\vn{abs.cont.fpg.js.lo}{0.24}$--$\vn{abs.cont.fpg.js.hi}{0.41}$ nats, success $\vn{abs.cont.fpg.succ.lo}{0.92}$--$\vn{abs.cont.fpg.succ.hi}{0.99}$), but it reaches the goal without staying: only $\vn{s12.fpg.fig_pct.lo}{6}$--$\vn{s12.fpg.fig_pct.hi}{14}\%$ of its rollouts end there, against $\vn{s12.exp.fig_pct.lo}{72}$--$\vn{s12.exp.fig_pct.hi}{76}\%$ for the expert.
In the finite mazes the FB models are provably approximate, because the rank of the transition kernel exceeds the representation rank, and their measured successor-measure errors are large.
We report every seed, the cost of every phase, and the limits of three-seed evidence.

\paragraph{Note on tools and on the evidence.}
The source code, frozen configurations, and reproduction instructions for this memoir are publicly available at \url{https://github.com/AliJavanJ1/il-memoir-reproduction}.
The code, the proof compendium and this text were drafted with the help of an AI assistant (Claude, Anthropic) under the author's direction.
The tables of the results sections are generated from the saved run files by \texttt{report/verify/make\_tables.py}.
Every number of the text that is computed from the runs, from the environment and audit checks or from a closed-form expression is typeset through a macro that names a registered claim, and \texttt{report/verify/verify\_results.py} checks that the printed literal is the registered one and that it equals the value recomputed from the saved files at the printed precision.
Configuration settings quoted in the text are checked against the frozen configuration, but most of their occurrences are not tied to these checks (\cref{app:repro}).
No theorem in this memoir is a statement about the convergence of neural-network training or about the optimality of an approximate model.
