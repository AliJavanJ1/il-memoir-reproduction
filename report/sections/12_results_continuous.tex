\section{Results in the continuous maze}\label{sec:results-continuous}

This section answers Q5.
In the continuous four-rooms maze the state is a position in $[0,1]^2$ and the actions are the same five moves.
FB uses the DQN-style approximation of the state--action space: the forward network maps a raw position and a latent to one $d$-dimensional vector per action, as a DQN maps a state to one value per action, and the policy is a softmax over the five scores $F(s,a,z)^\top z$. $f$-PG uses a categorical MLP policy and a kernel density estimate of the occupancies.
No exact oracle exists here: every number is a Monte Carlo estimate from 500 fresh rollouts per checkpoint, and the occupancy divergence is the plug-in histogram estimate of \cref{sec:protocol}.

\Cref{tab:continuous} and \cref{fig:final-core} give the final policies.
\textbf{No method meets the operational thresholds in the continuous maze, at any checkpoint of any run} (\cref{tab:convergence}).
The expert rows show that this is not an artefact of the estimator: the expert's own divergence is $\vn{s12.floor.lo}{0.0005}$ to $\vn{s12.floor.hi}{0.0013}$ nats, \vn{s12.orders}{two} orders of magnitude below the learners'.

\paragraph{$f$-PG} ends closest to the expert, with a divergence of $\vn{s12.fpg.js.lo}{0.24}$ to $\vn{s12.fpg.js.hi}{0.41}$ nats, and reaches the goal in $\vn{s12.fpg.succ_pct.lo}{92}$ to $\vn{s12.fpg.succ_pct.hi}{99}\%$ of the rollouts in all \vn{n.runs.cont.word}{nine} runs.
But it does not imitate the expert's behaviour at the goal: only $\vn{s12.fpg.fig_pct.lo}{6}$ to $\vn{s12.fpg.fig_pct.hi}{14}\%$ of its rollouts end in the goal region, against $\vn{s12.exp.fig_pct.lo}{72}$ to $\vn{s12.exp.fig_pct.hi}{76}\%$ for the expert, and after the first hit it spends $\vn{s12.fpg.post_pct.lo}{10}$ to $\vn{s12.fpg.post_pct.hi}{18}\%$ of the remaining steps there, against $\vn{s12.exp.post_pct.lo}{77}$ to $\vn{s12.exp.post_pct.hi}{79}\%$.
Its policy remains very stochastic (mean entropy $\vn{s12.fpg.ent.lo}{1.04}$ to $\vn{s12.fpg.ent.hi}{1.47}$ nats on visited states, against $\log5\approx\vn{const.log5}{1.61}$ for the uniform policy), and its divergence was still decreasing at the end of the budget: over the last $\vn{fpg.interval.c}{10{,}000}$ learner transitions it fell by $\vn{s12.fpg.dec.lo}{0.003}$ to $\vn{s12.fpg.dec.hi}{0.039}$ nats ($\vn{s12.fpg.dec_mean}{0.025}$ on average).
Together with a learning rate at the edge of the tested grid (\cref{sec:m-pilot}), this suggests that the budget, not an obstacle we can identify, limited $f$-PG here; the runs do not show where it would have stopped.

\paragraph{\ERFB} reaches the goal in $\vn{s12.er.succ_pct.lo}{12}$ to $\vn{s12.er.succ_pct.hi}{99}\%$ of the rollouts (at least $80\%$ in \vn{s12.er.n08}{4} of the \vn{n.runs.cont}{9} runs) but almost never stays: at most $\vn{s12.er.fig_max_pct}{0.4}\%$ of its rollouts end in the goal region. Its divergence is $\vn{s12.er.js.lo}{0.48}$ to $\vn{s12.er.js.hi}{0.66}$ nats.

\paragraph{\DMFB} has divergences of $\vn{s12.dm.js.lo}{0.35}$ to $\vn{s12.dm.js.hi}{0.69}$ nats and success $\vn{s12.dm.succ.lo}{0}$ to $\vn{s12.dm.succ.hi}{0.88}$ (at least $0.80$ in \vn{s12.dm.n08}{2} runs). Its largest divergence, $\vn{s12.dm.max}{0.693}$ nats, is essentially the maximum $\log2$ of $\JS$, reached when the learner's rollouts and the expert's occupy disjoint histogram cells.
Paired with \ERFB{} by goal and seed, \DMFB{} has a larger divergence in \vn{s12.pair.js}{4} of the \vn{s12.pair.n}{9} runs and a lower success in \vn{s12.pair.succ}{7}; its latent steps increased the divergence in \vn{s12.dmpath.js}{4} of the \vn{abs.dm.n_cont}{9} adaptations (\cref{fig:dm-path}).

\subsection{Learning curves}

$f$-PG starts from a nearly uniform policy at $\vn{s12.fpg.js0.lo}{0.57}$--$\vn{s12.fpg.js0.hi}{0.59}$ nats. Averaged over the \vn{n.runs.cont.word}{nine} runs, its success rises from $\vn{s12.fpg.succ0}{0.29}$ at the start to $\vn{s12.fpg.succ20k}{0.81}$ after $\vn{s12.fpg.x20k}{20{,}000}$ transitions and $\vn{s12.fpg.succ_end}{0.97}$ at the end, while its mean divergence decreases at every checkpoint (\cref{fig:fpg-curves}, right).
\ERFB{} and \DMFB{} change little with pretraining: at every snapshot, their mean divergence over the \vn{n.runs.cont.word}{nine} runs lies between $\vn{s12.fbil.snapmean.lo}{0.51}$ and $\vn{s12.fbil.snapmean.hi}{0.56}$ nats (\cref{fig:fbil-snapshots}, right). Between consecutive snapshots the divergence of a run changes by up to $\vn{s12.swing}{0.31}$ nats, and \vn{s12.lost}{5} of the \vn{s12.curves}{18} curves reach a success of $0.80$ at an earlier snapshot but not at the last.

\subsection{The FB model with a DQN-style forward network}

The replay data contained every pair of a free maze-lattice cell (side $1/7$) and an action at least \vn{s12.cover}{423} times.
At the resolution of the \vn{env.hist_cells}{356} cells of the evaluation histogram, coverage is incomplete near walls: the smallest number of visits of a pair of a histogram cell and an action is \vn{s12.hcov.min.s0}{0}, \vn{s12.hcov.min.s1}{1} and \vn{s12.hcov.min.s2}{2} in the buffers of seeds 0, 1 and 2, and \vn{s12.hcov.low.lo}{29} to \vn{s12.hcov.low.hi}{34} pairs per buffer have fewer than \vn{s12.hcov.low_threshold}{10} visits, all in histogram cells of which walls leave only $\vn{s12.hcov.free_pct}{14}\%$ free (counts from the stored buffers by \nolinkurl{report/verify/replay_hist_coverage.py}).
Neither count rules out coverage problems of the continuous state space at finer scales.
The learned backward representation is nearly degenerate (\cref{tab:fbdiag}): the smallest eigenvalue of its Gram matrix under the replay measure is $\vn{s12.lmin.lo}{1.7\times10^{-5}}$ to $\vn{s12.lmin.hi}{4.2\times10^{-5}}$, against $\vn{s11.lmin.lo}{0.93}$ to $\vn{s11.lmin.hi}{0.96}$ in the finite mazes, while the largest is $\vn{s12.lmax.lo}{2.0}$ to $\vn{s12.lmax.hi}{2.2}$ and $\lVert\hat G_B-I\rVert_F$ is about $\vn{s12.dev}{3.4}$.
For the eigenvector $u$ of $\lambda_{\min}$, $\int(u^\top B)^2\dd\hat\nu=\lambda_{\min}$: at least one direction of $B$ is almost zero on the replay data, despite the orthonormality penalty that pushes $\hat G_B$ towards the identity. The diagnostics record only the smallest and the largest eigenvalue, so this report does not say how many directions are poorly excited.
The pilot had already shown this ($\lambda_{\min}\approx\vn{s10.pilot.lmin}{2\times10^{-5}}$ at all three temperatures); the protocol was frozen and the settings were not changed in response.
We could not compute the model error exactly here, so we cannot say how much of the failure comes from the representation and how much from the latent step.
Plausible causes that these runs do not test are the raw $(x,y)$ inputs to $B$ (FB21 used radial-basis features in its continuous maze), the budget of $\vn{fb.updates.c}{20{,}000}$ updates, and the rank $d=16$.

\paragraph{Answer for Q5, in short.}
With a DQN-style forward network, FB pretraining on $\vn{replay.c}{100{,}000}$ task-blind transitions produced a backward representation with a nearly singular Gram matrix, and neither \ERFB{} nor \DMFB{} imitated the expert: both sometimes reach the goal but neither stays.
$f$-PG with a KDE signal and an MLP policy made steady progress but did not reach the thresholds within $\vn{budget.fpg.c}{100{,}000}$ learner transitions.
These are statements about this maze, these budgets and three seeds.
