\section{Method map: from each update to its result and its code}\label{app:methodmap}

This appendix condenses the project's method map (\nolinkurl{METHOD_MAP.md}).
For every implemented step it gives the formula, the result that justifies it, its status, and the function that implements it.
\emph{Identity}: a definition or exact identity that the code reproduces up to rounding, checked by a test against a closed form.
\emph{Proved}: a theorem whose hypotheses the implementation meets by construction.
\emph{Conditional}: proved only under hypotheses that the trained system does not meet or cannot verify; an interpretation, never a guarantee.
\emph{Heuristic}: no theorem (optimizers, step sizes, target networks, projections, plug-in estimates).
The approximation codes in the last column refer to \cref{tab:approx-register}.

\subsection{Data access}

Learners receive the simulator (actions in, next observation out), the learner view of the configuration, the demonstration \emph{states} and, for FB, the task-blind replay buffer.
They never receive the finite transition matrix, the continuous kernel parameters, the evaluation reward, the goal, the success rule, the expert actions or the held-out evaluation trajectories, and \ERFB{} and \DMFB{} have no environment handle at all.
The maze walls are passed to the density estimators only, to normalize the KDE and the histogram reference law on the free space.
These rules are enforced statically (\nolinkurl{tests/test_no_leakage.py} rejects the forbidden imports and names in the learner modules and training executors) and behaviourally: scrambling the stored expert actions, or changing the reward width, goal radius and success rule, leaves every training output bit-identical (\nolinkurl{tests/test_pipeline.py}).
Evaluation transitions are generated by the evaluator with their own random streams, counted, and never written where a learner reads.

\subsection{Evaluation (\texttt{il\_memoir/evaluation.py})}

In the finite mazes the oracle computes, from $P$, the exact held-out divergence $\JS(d^+_{\pi,H},q_E)$, the state--action divergence, the $H$-step return and its gap to $V^\star_H$ (\cref{prop:occ-return,prop:occ-hstep}), the exact hit probability and the probability of ending in the goal, and the FB model error of \cref{thm:fb-approx}; 200 rollouts cross-check them.
In the continuous maze, 500 rollouts per checkpoint give the success rate, the return, the goal-holding fractions and the plug-in histogram divergence against $2{,}000$ reference rollouts (S10).
Evaluation reads each policy through the same function the learner uses, checks every checkpoint file against the hash recorded by its training job, and writes nothing a learner reads.
